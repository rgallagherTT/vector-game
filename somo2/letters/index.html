      For grown-ups

      Start with these letters

      satipn

      The tracing center teaches letters in this order, starting with s, a, t, i, p, n. These letters make easy words, so your child can read real words quickly: sat, sit, tap, tip, pin, pan, nap, nip, sip. Then add c, k, e, h, r, m, d, and carry on from there.

      Start tracing s

      For each new letter

      1. Listen. In Phonics, pick the letter. Tap Hear the letter and Hear the word, and say the sound together.
      2. Trace. In the tracing center, follow the red dots and arrows. Say the steps out loud, like "a round circle, then a line down."
      3. Find it. Look for the letter in a book or around the house, and say its sound.
      4. Play. Try Safari Sound Match, and take the quick check every few days to see what to practice.

      Good habits

      • Keep it short. Five to ten minutes a day is plenty, so stop while it is still fun.
      • Teach the sound first, then the letter's name. Say sounds short and clear.
      • Begin with lowercase letters, because most words in books use them. Add capitals when your child is ready, starting with the first letter of their name.
      • Praise effort, not perfect tracing. If it is tricky, tap Start over and try again together.
      • Read aloud together every day. Use Read to me, then read the page again and point to the words. Ask which words rhyme.
      • Use your home language too. Talk about sounds and words in Swahili, English or your mother tongue.
      • After tracing on the screen, try writing the letter in the air, in sand or on paper.
